Open `13-heat-lane-a.png` and `13-heat-lane-b.png` side by side: the one-door plane is a clean front-to-back gradient that gets to near-black at row 30, and the two-door plane is dark in the middle and pale at both ends, because both ends are exits. The green fix works too and I can prove it with a pixel census instead of vibes: at t=1:12 the two-doors lane is 2.199% saturated green and the free-for-all lane is 0.141%, a **15.6x** difference, so the aisle now visibly looks like a road or a parking lot depending on which strategy you picked. The share link reproduced a 777 at 100% load with legacy bins and 34% distracted, in a cold context, to the second. Personal best is a margin now and it remembers which strategy earned it. Eleven of the seventeen things I yelled about in round two are dead.

And then I switched the aircraft dropdown to a 777 and the heat map told on you. The top seat block is pale and the bottom seat block is near-black, and it is not seed noise: **on every twin-aisle aircraft in your list, one aisle takes between 1.7x and 2.0x as long as the other.** 777: 236 s versus 477 s. Seat C waits 212 s, seat J waits 540 s, same plane, same run. Round two you fixed the left/right bias by drawing a `priority` instead of sorting on passenger id, and then you left `processWalkers` iterating `for (aisleIndex = 0; aisleIndex < state.aisles.length; ...)` against a single shared front-door server, so aisle 0 gets first refusal on the door on every single step forever. It is the exact same bug with a different index, your new fairness test only ever runs the A320, and your flagship feature renders the result as an unmissable seam down the middle of the widebody. While I was in the dropdown I also found that **two doors is a net loss on two of your nine presets** — E175 3.27m to 3.61m, high-density 737 10.45m to 11.47m, forty seeds each — because tight bins scatter bags away from the seat and `doorCell` is chosen from the seat row without ever asking where the bag is. Your headline result silently inverts one click away and nothing on the page says so. Meanwhile the very first thing a visitor sees is Free-for-all versus Aisle first, which is a 59/41 coin flip with a **three second** median margin, and your own finding sentence admits it in print: "Free-for-all and Aisle first finish within seconds of each other." You open on the tie and hide the 2:16 behind a dropdown. Fix the door server, fix the default matchup, and make the worst-seat label legible — right now it is paper-coloured text stroked with a paper-coloured halo, which is exactly as readable as it sounds — and this is a thing I would actually send to people.

## New findings

### BLOCKER

**NEW3-B1. On every twin-aisle aircraft, one aisle takes twice as long as the other. The shared front door always serves aisle 0 first.**

Mean total time aboard per passenger, 10 seeds, free-for-all, defaults:

| preset | layout | aisle 0 | aisle 1 | penalty |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 767 | 2-3-2 | 165 s (n=948) | **276 s** (n=722) | +67% |
| 787 | 3-3-3 | 202 s (n=1271) | **366 s** (n=1029) | +81% |
| 777 | 3-4-3 | 236 s (n=1525) | **477 s** (n=1535) | **+102%** |

By column on the 777 the discontinuity is surgical, and it lands exactly where the middle block splits between the two aisles:

```
A=272  B=238  C=212  D=220  E=241  ‖  F=494  G=435  H=431  I=483  J=540
```

Seat C waits 212 s. Seat J waits 540 s. Same aircraft, same run, 2.5x.

Root cause, two files:

- `aisle.js:createDoorServers()` — "The front door is one server shared across every aisle." One `busyUntil` scalar for the whole aircraft.
- `deplane-walk.js:29` — `for (let aisleIndex = 0; aisleIndex < state.aisles.length; aisleIndex += 1)`. Aisle 0 is processed first on every step, so whenever the door is free, aisle 0's door-cell walker calls `doorTryAdmit` first and wins. Aisle 1 only ever gets the door on a step when aisle 0 has nobody queued.

This is round-02's NEW-M2 with the index renamed. You fixed contested *seat* cells by replacing an id sort with a `priority` draw, and never touched the shared door server. Worse, the fairness test you added to guard the fix (`left-half and right-half median exit times differ by under 5 s at defaults`) runs on the **A320 only**, so it is green precisely where the bug is absent.

And the heat view broadcasts it. `30-preset-b777-finish.png`: the top seat block is pale tan top-to-bottom and the bottom block is near-black. That is the seam I warned you about in round two, on the one screenshot this project most wants people to post.

**Fix:** the shared door must alternate between aisles, not favour index 0. Round-robin the aisle order per step, or draw it from the step rng the way you now draw seat-cell ties. Then move the fairness test onto a widebody, comparing per-aisle medians, not per-half on a single-aisle plane. It is a handful of lines and it is the difference between a simulation and a queueing artifact.

### MAJOR

**NEW3-M1. Two doors is a net loss on two of your nine presets, and nothing says so.**

40 seeds per cell, CLI, defaults:

| preset | free-for-all | two doors | two doors saves |
|---|---|---|---|
| CRJ-700 | 3.03m | 2.94m | 3% |
| **E175** | 3.27m | **3.61m** | **-10%** |
| 717 | 5.35m | 5.17m | 3% |
| A320 | 6.39m | 4.13m | 35% |
| **737-800 high-density** | 10.45m | **11.47m** | **-10%** |
| 777 | 10.57m | 5.71m | 46% |

Root cause is bin capacity, isolated by sweep. Layout 3-3, 33 rows, varying `binCapacityPerSeatRow`:

```
cap 1.5 : ffa 6.25m  two-doors 3.79m   +39%
cap 1.0 : ffa 6.62m  two-doors 4.15m   +37%
cap 0.8 : ffa 8.54m  two-doors 7.69m   +10%
cap 0.67: ffa 9.78m  two-doors 10.44m   -7%      <- b738-hd
cap 0.5 : ffa 8.55m  two-doors 9.33m    -9%
```

Row count is not the cause (3-3 at 33 rows with normal bins still saves 37%). Tight bins push bags far from their owner, and `deplane-sim.js:136` sets `passenger.doorCell = nearestDoorCell(cabin, passenger.row)` from the **seat row alone**, never asking where the bag ended up. A rear-door passenger whose bag overflowed twelve rows forward walks forward, retrieves, then pays `counterflowExtraSecondsPerRow` all the way back. Nobody on a real aeroplane does that; they use the door nearest their bag.

The consequence on screen: the explainer, the blurb and the compare chart all sell two doors as the big win, and one click in the aircraft dropdown silently inverts it with no explanation anywhere.

**Fix:** choose `doorCell` from the passenger's final bag position, not their seat row, and re-derive. If after that the inversion survives on tight bins, it is genuinely the most interesting result in the whole toy and belongs in the finding sentence, not buried.
